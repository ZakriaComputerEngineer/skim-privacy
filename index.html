Skim Browser Privacy Policy

Last updated October 18, 2026

I built Skim because I wanted a browser that doesn't watch what I do. This page explains, in plain words, what Skim keeps on your computer and when it talks to the internet.

In short

Skim doesn't have accounts, analytics or telemetry. I don't collect anything about you, so there's nothing for me to sell or share. Your history, bookmarks and settings live on your own PC and nowhere else.

What stays on your computer

Like any browser, Skim saves a few things locally so it can work properly. None of it is sent to me.

Private windows keep everything in memory and throw it away when you close them. If you'd like, Skim can also wipe your cookies, history and cache every time you quit. You'll find that option in Settings under Shields & privacy.

When Skim goes online

Apart from loading the websites you open, Skim only connects to the internet for two things:

  • Search suggestions. As you type in the address bar, your text goes to the search engine you picked (DuckDuckGo unless you change it) so it can suggest searches. Your cookies aren't sent along. You can switch suggestions off in Settings under Search.
  • Blocklist updates. Every so often Skim downloads the public ad and tracker lists it uses, such as EasyList and EasyPrivacy, from Ghostery's servers. Nothing about you is included in these downloads.

Skim also sends the Global Privacy Control signal to every site you visit, which tells them you don't want your data sold or shared.

Websites and search engines

The websites you visit have their own privacy policies and may still collect information about you. Shields stop a lot of trackers and third party cookies, but honestly no browser can block everything. Searches are handled by your chosen search engine under its own policy.

Children

Skim is a general purpose browser. It doesn't collect information from anyone, children included.
